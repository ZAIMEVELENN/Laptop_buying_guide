# Laptop_buying_guide
Small decription that should be checked when concidering to buy a new laptop

**Brand**: Lenovo > Apple > Dell > ASUS > HP (I use HP)

**RAM**: 16 GB and up (32 GB is the sweet spot)

**Storage**: 476Gib/512GB is perfect. The higher is better but make sure you can actually use it and not let the space sitting to gather dust

**CPU**: AMD Ryzen 7 / Intel Core Ultra 7 (sweet spot), or AMD Ryzen 5 / Intel Core i5 / Core Ultra 5 — nothing older than 5 years

**GPU** (NVIDIA): RTX 4050 and up. Best is RTX 4070 (or up to the 50-series) if you want to run local AI.
A single GPU works best with Linux.
Dual GPUs are best for performance, though they can introduce a few issues on Linux; Windows is mostly fine.

**Extra Things to check before buying**:
**Screen resolution & size**: Big for your eyes, or small so it's easy to travel with. 16:10 or 16:9 at least 144hz

**Keyboard quality**
**Ports**: Check whether they suit your needs (USB Type-C, HDMI, Ethernet, etc.).

**Battery life**: Check how it performs—some have a short lifespan with crazy performance, while others have a long lifespan but are slower.

**Weight**: Check whether you can carry it comfortably (some are heavy, though their performance is crazy good).

**Upgradability**: Decide whether you want to upgrade your laptop's RAM, SSD, or Wi-Fi card. If you want that option, look for parts that are not soldered to the motherboard.

**Note**: Soldered RAM/CPU offers better performance than upgradable parts, but the laptop may become outdated or slow over time until you have no choice but to buy a new one. If the laptop is upgradable, you can upgrade it as you use it.


**Note that this a matter of personal opinion. Anyone else can think otherwise. Everyone can be more accurate and have better opinion than I.**
